# Thesis writing preferences

This profile applies to the Indonesian thesis in this workspace. Evidence comes from `thesis_writing_taste_all.csv`. Record numbers below count parsed CSV records, excluding the header. The file has no timestamps or acceptance column. Interpretations below distinguish explicit instructions from proposed rewrites.

## Explain the relationship

Name the object and comparison. Explain which data are counted and what the status describes. Avoid leaving the reader to infer what “hasil,” “berbeda,” or “berlaku” refers to. Records 27–35 reject these ambiguities.

Prefer ordinary precise verbs and established terminology. Explain the operation before introducing an abstract label. Records 5, 107–111, and 120 reject “kelompok analisis,” stages acting as people, “unit penilaian,” and unexplained statistical labels in their respective passages. These are contextual objections, not universal word bans.

Include detail that explains a result. Record 36 explicitly praises a detailed account of rounding and the SPM/SLA threshold. A long paragraph may be the right explanation. Record 2 praises a case overview with concrete data conditions.

Connect sentences through their actual relationship. Record 71 asks the author to identify an example as an example. Record 72 requests integration with the preceding sentence. Add causal language only when the source supports causation.

## Choose what belongs

Give each paragraph a purpose. Keep detail that supports that purpose. Explain controlled conditions in their appropriate methods passage, then avoid repeating them mechanically. Records 1, 73, 116, and 119 support this distinction.

Keep useful orientation. Record 18 requests an introduction to the conclusion. Record 3 rejects a different introduction that sounds like an editing prompt. Neither establishes a rule to always add or always remove introductions.

Keep general explanations with general concepts and study procedures with methods. Records 123–127 object to placing shared concepts or study details under individual model descriptions. A true fact can still be misplaced.

State the actual procedure. Record 122 rejects a disclaimer about an unrequested alternative validator. Preserve limitations and exclusions that change interpretation, even when their wording needs repair.

Keep technical English such as tool, tool call, prompt, array, and context window when the manuscript uses the exact concept. Record 139 requests tool call. Preserve model names, field names, and distinctions among null, false, zero, absent, and not applicable.

Use expected result for the established reference answer when appropriate. Record 87 questions “acuan,” but its replacement concerns unrelated front matter. Treat that replacement as unreliable evidence, not a reusable example.

Record 91 asks whether to use task or tugas. Keep the established manuscript choice unless a later instruction settles it. Record 74 requests memiliki for a particular occurrence of memperoleh. Do not apply a global replacement.

## Respect document choices

Record 121 bans semicolons in thesis prose. Rewrite naturally. Preserve verbatim research artifacts and report any conflict separately rather than silently altering a prompt, code fragment, or quotation.

Records 12–14 request prose for a single objective or question. Records 15–16 request a glossary and explanation of case identifiers. Records 20 and 24 request displayed equations. Records 21–23 and 26 concern captions, boxed prompts, and layout. Verify those in the actual document application when editing that artifact. Markdown alone does not establish page-layout correctness.

Record 104 objects to antarpengulangan but does not establish a universal replacement. “Pada setiap pengulangan” or “antara pengulangan” may express the intended relationship naturally.
