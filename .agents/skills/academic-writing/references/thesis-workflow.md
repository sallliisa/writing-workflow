# Thesis workflow

## Use a supplied reference thesis

When the user requests a friend's thesis as a reference, inspect its actual subsection order and the purpose of each subsection. Follow useful organizational habits while preserving the current thesis's argument and institutional requirements.

Verify claims, citations, and definitions before reusing them. Keep the reference's datasets, model choices, results, and wording separate. If the reference is unavailable, state that limitation only when it affects the requested comparison.

## Assign chapter responsibilities

- The introduction establishes the concrete problem, research questions, scope, and contributions the design can support.
- The literature review defines concepts needed later and explains relevant published work.
- The methods make inputs, actions, outputs, controlled conditions, and evaluation units traceable.
- The results report findings with the denominators and conditions needed to interpret them. Discussion explains relationships beyond restating tables.
- The conclusion answers the research questions. Recommendations follow from findings, limitations, or unresolved questions.

Preserve the requested chapter structure. A short subsection can remain short when the finding and table reference complete its purpose.

## Build useful comparison tables

Use comparable attributes with verified values, units, dates, and conditions. Verify model variants, price categories, and benchmark versions when those attributes are requested. Omit promotional descriptions and artificial distinctions. Keep qualifications that change a value's meaning. A source line can carry attribution without repeating it in each cell.

## Explain evaluation methods

Describe the actual evaluation procedure and its units. Include parsing, ordering, numeric comparison, and failure handling where they affect reproducibility. Support the published evaluation principle with relevant research, while identifying operational rules chosen for this study.

Explain each comparison separately when several factors are involved. Name the groups and describe how their answers enter the comparison. Preserve whether results are pooled across conditions or compared within one condition. Use explicit objects rather than compressed labels that the reader must decode.

Apply the relevance and evidence checks in the entrypoint before adding details. Preserve the distinction between documenting an actual procedure and denying an unused alternative.
