# Academic writing

Write formal, explanatory Indonesian that a reader can understand on the first reading. Follow the requested language, scope, and artifact format.

For this thesis, read the [thesis profile](../thesis-revision/references/thesis-profile.md). For chapter organization, a supplied reference thesis, comparison tables, or methodological explanations, read [thesis workflow](references/thesis-workflow.md). For substantial rewriting, read the [decision examples](references/examples.md). Examples illustrate decisions rather than sentence templates.

## Establish the passage's job

Read enough surrounding text to identify the reader question, central claim, evidence, and reason for including the passage here. Distinguish definitions, published findings, local research choices, procedures, observations, and interpretations. Explain shared background where it belongs rather than presenting it as a distinctive property of one model or method.

Choose whether to keep, rewrite, move, or remove a passage before polishing its wording. A true, cited sentence can still be irrelevant or repetitive. Preserve a useful introduction, transition, interpretation, or qualification when it helps the reader follow the argument.

Edit only when you can name the reader problem. Keep clear passages. Past revisions supply evidence of preferences, not a standing instruction to keep rewriting. An `after` entry in an editing log is not evidence of approval by itself. Check linked feedback about the same passage before using it for calibration. Infer chronology only when the source establishes it.

## Recover and preserve the meaning

Identify the subject, action, object, comparison, and condition. Recover unclear relationships from the supplied sources. If the intended meaning remains uncertain, record the smallest missing fact outside the replacement prose.

Preserve the population, unit, period, denominator, exception, and uncertainty that affect the claim. Distinguish source-data rows, reports, incidents, actions, cases, executions, and answers when they represent different things. Keep exact identifiers and meaningful status distinctions, including false, null, absent, zero, and not applicable.

Keep quotations and experimental artifacts exact. Report conflicts between source integrity and formatting preferences separately. A language edit cannot silently correct a disputed fact, invent an operational rule, or turn an author's assurance into independent verification.

## Explain the relationship plainly

Use familiar precise verbs and concrete research objects. Name what is counted, compared, connected, tested, or provided. Replace an abstract label with its referent when the label makes the reader reconstruct the operation.

Choose active or passive voice according to the focus. Avoid third-person self-reference with "peneliti" as the routine narrative subject. Preserve attribution when it identifies who supplied or verified a claim.

Retain established technical English when it identifies the concept more clearly. Follow the manuscript's terminology rather than rotating synonyms. Explain unfamiliar terms at their first substantive use. Apply the institution's conventions for spelling, italics, acronyms, and citations.

Give each paragraph one coherent job. Split or combine sentences only when doing so makes their relationship easier to understand. Sentence length, paragraph length, rhythm, and the number of sentences are not quality targets. A longer explanation can be necessary.

Use transitions for a real sequence, comparison, qualification, or supported consequence. Make comparison objects identifiable. Add an example, calculation, or exception only when it answers a relevant reader question. Keep editorial concerns outside the manuscript.

End the paragraph when its explanation is complete. Keep a closing sentence when it adds necessary information. Remove automatic summaries, unraised alternatives, staged contrasts such as "bukan X, melainkan Y," and repeated disclaimers when they add no distinction. Preserve genuine negatives and comparisons that change interpretation.

## Protect the evidence

Keep citations attached to their supported claims when splitting or moving text. Preserve cited mechanisms and qualifications. Verify new citations against the source before adding them. A source on the same topic does not necessarily support the particular relationship being claimed.

Distinguish published principles from research-specific operational choices. A comparable method can support an approach without validating every local implementation detail. Flag unsupported retained claims as well as new claims. Keep these notes outside clean prose.

Match certainty to the evidence. Preserve association rather than introducing causation. Use statistical significance only when a supported analysis establishes it. An empirical observation does not by itself justify "membuktikan" or a general guarantee.

Check numbers, arithmetic, units, dates, labels, denominators, and rounding. Preserve the difference between percentage points and relative percentage change. When a claim is removed or relocated, account for its associated evidence rather than retaining detached citations or numbers.

## Review and deliver

Compare the replacement with the source. Check meaning, section placement, conditions, claim scope, uncertainty, evidence, numbers, technical objects, and cross-references. Then read for comprehension, natural wording, continuity, and unnecessary detail.

Deliver the requested prose, review, or comparison. Separate factual questions from language edits. A review request alone does not authorize rewriting the entire document. Apply recurring feedback across the authorized scope. When saving edits, inspect the saved artifact.

Stop when the identified problems are resolved without a material regression. Keep the source when a proposed edit offers no clear improvement. Make no claims about authorship or AI-detector outcomes.
